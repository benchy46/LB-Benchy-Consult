## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed

- **Process should scale, not just solve today's instance** — evident in the SOP torque-code decision ("this is for scalability and standardization") and the part-numbering logic (wants a rule that will keep working as more colour variants appear), not one-off fixes.
- **Ownership over hand-waving** — pushes for leads to personally assemble hardware before sign-off (docking station), rather than trusting drawings/specs alone.
- **Willing to name supplier/vendor problems directly and propose the fix**, not just flag them (Golden Scale ban + parallel-sourcing proposal).
- **Consistently advocates for junior/intern headcount** as a lever the team underuses, citing specific past examples rather than abstract argument.
- **Adopts AI tooling early and pushes teammates to do the same** (Solidworks MCP rollout, Claude-generated procurement workflows, recommending Claude for self-review of communication style) — a hands-on, "try it yourself first" approach to new tools rather than mandating from a distance.
- **Comfortable being blunt about internal friction** (tedious approval flows, chronically late suppliers) but channels it into a proposed process fix rather than just a complaint.
- **Understated, low-friction personal communication** — brief, factual updates about leave/family circumstances; heavy use of short acknowledgments in logistics threads, reserving longer writing for substantive technical/process points.
- **Pushes filtering and prioritisation upward, capture downward** *(added 2026-08-17)* — he asks the team to log a lot, but consistently resists making them decide what is worth logging. Reviewer-count reduction (2025-08-12), "Whatever updates you have will do" (2026-07-28, `s3-mech-architecture`), and the 2026-08-16 high-impact-filtering pushback are all the same trade: keep the cost of contributing near zero, and put any judgment that needs org-wide context on the people who have it.
