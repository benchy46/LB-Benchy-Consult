## Decisions & Reasoning (Benjamin)

- **Part numbering convention for colour-only variants** — when RAL colour changes but the mechanical design doesn't, Benjamin proposed and then settled (with Lucas/Daniel) on: SKU, Engineering Part Number, and Rev number *all* change and Rev resets to 01, with the drawing remarks noting "same design/mold as [old SKU]." Reasoning: an EPN/Rev needs to unambiguously signal "engineering design changed" vs "only cosmetic/colour changed," so future engineers can tell at a glance whether tooling is actually different. "Oh boy who knew changing colour would be this difficult" — 2024-11-11. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1731290997061409) (thread: p1731292993252159)
- **SOP torque notation standard (brass inserts)** — introduced a scalability rule: SOPs reference a coded label ("TMx_Brass") rather than a literal torque value, "for scalability and standardization," and mandated the same notation for the upcoming VP25 product. Explicitly flagged that non-brass insert types will need their own convention later. 2024-11-07. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1730972178021039)
- **SOP process ownership handoff to Production** — decided that once production reviews/trials the template SOP standard (target: 25 April 2025), production gets "full jurisdiction" over the SOP standard going forward, with engineering deferring to their format for all future SOPs. 2025-04-02. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1743579209914769)
- **Intern staffing as a standing recommendation** — repeatedly and strongly advocated for a minimum of 1–2 interns per active project/product at all times, citing concrete past ROI (Hendrik freeing up his time on control hub/sourcing; naming specific admin/repetitive tasks — DHL shipping admin, removing 3DP supports, crimping, wire/tube length measurement, prelim system/robot testing). Flagged "R5 no intern, really not ideal" as a real gap. 2025-09-05, echoed and expanded by Pradeep Kumar the next day. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1757082779978329)
- **Reduce design-review bottleneck** — agreed with Daniel/Lucas to cut required reviewers for Lead-level designs from "everyone" down to 2 reviewers (3 for Senior-and-below designs), specifically so work isn't blocked waiting for full-team availability, while still guaranteeing at least 3 leads' eyes across a design's lifecycle. 2025-08-12. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1754964090189909)
- **Docking station: mandate leads self-assemble before release** — "I want to mandate that we leads sit and assemble the docking station first before we release anything," after finding assembly problems himself on the bilge pump side. Reasoning: catch integration/assembly issues before they reach production/customers, not just review drawings on paper. 2026-01-23. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1769164214340689)
- **Ban a chronically late supplier without backup** — "I want to Hard Ban using Golden scale without backup for all future builds, especially when timeline is at risk. They never once delivered on time." Cited repeated late deliveries across DV02 and DV03 builds as the pattern that led to this call, and proposed the fix (parallel-source small quantities from a reliable supplier like Kaier) rather than just complaining. 2025-12-19. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1766157333383409)
- **Push for a formal escalation channel on communication practice** — raised to Sunardi Tay that the org conflates "problem definition" with "solution proposals," causing engineers to waste time re-deriving the actual problem when stakeholders jump straight to a proposed fix. Proposed a mandatory intake form (deeper questions: is this a sales blocker, what's the customer workflow, what do competitors do, etc.) before requests are entertained — while explicitly noting "unfortunately i dont have the power to enforce this." 2026-05-17. [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C05TUB6TRHP/p1779012106086769)

Shows a pattern of pushing internal tools toward "prevent the user from making a sizing/spec error" rather than just data storage. 2026-07-07/08. ## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed

- **Process should scale, not just solve today's instance** — evident in the SOP torque-code decision ("this is for scalability and standardization") and the part-numbering logic (wants a rule that will keep working as more colour variants appear), not one-off fixes.
- **Ownership over hand-waving** — pushes for leads to personally assemble hardware before sign-off (docking station), rather than trusting drawings/specs alone.
- **Willing to name supplier/vendor problems directly and propose the fix**, not just flag them (Golden Scale ban + parallel-sourcing proposal).
- **Consistently advocates for junior/intern headcount** as a lever the team underuses, citing specific past examples rather than abstract argument.
- **Adopts AI tooling early and pushes teammates to do the same** (Solidworks MCP rollout, Claude-generated procurement workflows, recommending Claude for self-review of communication style) — a hands-on, "try it yourself first" approach to new tools rather than mandating from a distance.
- **Comfortable being blunt about internal friction** (tedious approval flows, chronically late suppliers) but channels it into a proposed process fix rather than just a complaint.
- **Understated, low-friction personal communication** — brief, factual updates about leave/family circumstances; heavy use of short acknowledgments in logistics threads, reserving longer writing for substantive technical/process points.
