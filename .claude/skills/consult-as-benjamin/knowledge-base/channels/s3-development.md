## Decisions & Reasoning (Benjamin)

- **Defers a protocol decision to the domain owner after surfacing the option** — noticed the sonar had RS485 and Modbus variants and asked whether either beat UART: "The sonar has RS485 and Modbus protocol variants. Would any one of those com methods be better instead of Uart?" Arun Kumar answered that RS485 is more robust over distance but no existing board supports it natively (UART-TTL only), it would force a PCB redesign, and it would make the sonar the only non-UART device (TOFs on S3 and lidar on R5 are all UART). Benjamin closed it immediately: "Okay got it, we will stick with uart." — a clean example of him raising an alternative from outside his discipline, then dropping it in one message once given a fleet-consistency argument. — 2026-07-31 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0A53C9L91S/p1785480287980239)

## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed

- **Cross-discipline questions framed as questions, not directives.** With autonomy and embedded he consistently asks what they need ("What is the sparse resolution that you need...", "Would any one of those com methods be better...") and accepts the answer; the directive register he uses in mech-only channels is largely absent here.
- **Fleet consistency beats per-part optimality** — he abandoned RS485 the moment it was framed as "the sonar would be the only non-UART device." Matches the torque-standardisation logic in `sustainingxproduction` (one value the factory can execute over a marginally better per-part spec).
- **Serviceability as a first-class architecture driver** — the castor decision is argued on "you'd have to flip the robot to change a brush," not on packaging or cost.
- **Brings external literature into hardware arguments** — citing a published paper on fiducial patterns rather than asserting from experience alone.
- **Names the meta-problem in-line** — "are we deciding if a feature should exist or are we discussing how to implement a feature?"
