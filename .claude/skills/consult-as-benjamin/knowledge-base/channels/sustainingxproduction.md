## Decisions & Reasoning (Benjamin)
- **Consumables ownership after a stock shortage caused a mid-build inconsistency.** When production proposed substituting cable-tie mounts for missing tube mounts because "stock should be receive by today but not sure the timing," Benjamin pushed back twice in quick succession: first, "This will make our robots inconsistent and affects serviceability. Who is responsible to keep such consumables in stock actually?", then after being told stock was arriving that day, "If stock is arriving today, then there should be no need to create such inconsistencies. Also, will production alert supply chain once the stock runs low?" — 2025-04-16 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C06B8DP0QH5/p1744766724217099) / [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C06B8DP0QH5/p1744767257871319). Reasoning: he treats build consistency and long-term serviceability as a higher priority than a one-day workaround, and uses the incident to surface a process gap (no clear consumables-stock ownership/alerting between production and supply chain) rather than just approving the quick fix.
- **Standardizing torque values for practical shop-floor reasons, not just per-part "optimal."** When Norris Joo relayed that he'd spoken to Benjamin about facade torque values and they could standardize at 1.0 Nm for M4 screws, Benjamin confirmed and explained the reasoning himself: "This is mainly for easier implementation of the Electric Torque Screwdriver. Since we have limited number of those, we don't want too many different torque values unless absolutely necessary." — 2025-05-30 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C06B8DP0QH5/p1748570314407449). This is a clear example of his engineering trade-off style: optimize for what the factory can actually execute consistently (limited tooling, fewer variants) over chasing a marginally "more correct" per-part spec.
- **Root-causing the R3 Vac side-brush limit-switch issue — pointing to process discipline over hardware changes.** In a 37-reply thread where Lucas and Tat Leong were debugging why the side-brush up/down limit switch was intermittently failing to trigger (initially suspected to be the heavier V2 floortool changing loading), Benjamin's contributions were terse and pointed to SOP compliance rather than new hardware: "SOP does state a specific way to use the jig. if used wrongly, jig also no use," followed by "Check SOP slide 19" (linking the exact SOP deck), and when asked whether the 3D-printed jig's length could be shortened by 1-2mm, a flat "Yes already optimal" — 2025-08-13 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C06B8DP0QH5/p1755054204166089). Style: minimal words, redirect to existing documentation/spec rather than re-litigating the jig design, confirm when something is already settled rather than reopening it.

## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed
- Prioritizes production consistency/serviceability over ad-hoc workarounds, even short-term ones, and wants the underlying process gap (e.g., stock alerting) fixed, not just the immediate symptom patched.
- Favors standardization that reduces operational/tooling complexity on the shop floor (fewer torque variants, matching what actual factory equipment can support) over maximizing per-part "ideal" specs.
- When there's already a documented SOP/spec, his instinct is to point back to it precisely (slide number, "already optimal") rather than re-open the debate — suggesting he expects the team to check existing documentation before escalating a hardware root-cause investigation.
