# #s3-handlebar
**Slack channel ID:** C0B2SFZAQKA
**Purpose:** Working channel dedicated to designing the S3 robot's handlebar assembly (hinge mechanism, extrusion profile, grip material, manufacturing) — created by Jack Dyer on 2026-05-07 from a conversation with Benjamin Chay, Benjamin Ang, and Hisham Baharin. This is Benjamin Chay's most active and voice-rich channel in this batch; he is effectively the design owner for the handlebar mechanism, working closely with Hisham Baharin (industrial design/CAD support).

Extrusion mold can keep. - **[2026-08-17] Split the functional floor from the finish upgrade on a coating price rise, then conceded to a durability argument — not to an aesthetic one.** Wei Hua proposed a matte rubber coating that would cut bubbles in the grip; it carried a 78.9% price increase, and the existing colour could only be customised at 3000 MOQ. Benjamin opened by refusing: "I'm not sure I'm willing to pay extra 78.9% increase in handlebar rubber coating price just to reduce the bubbles in the handle." When Jack Dyer asked whether dropping the rubber entirely would reintroduce static and heat concerns, and Hisham raised cold-climate grip, Benjamin separated the two questions immediately — the coating *itself* is non-negotiable ("Ya it definitely was an issue previously so, we kinda need to 100% have it, don't wanna regress"), while the *upgrade* had to justify itself. He pressure-tested it with a one-line reframe of the cost delta as a value question — "is it 78.9% nicer?" — and named the product position out loud: "idk man, we want cheap cheap robot so". Wei Hua then argued the matte finish would resist scratches and marks in service. That landed: "Hmm true that it is scratch resistant also. Well okay i buy that." — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1786959276258199)
  - Worth separating what did and did not move him. Wei Hua's strongest-sounding argument was positional ("HMI is ranked by sales as the #1 top design factor in sales success") and drew no response. The argument that flipped him was a **lifetime/durability** one — scratch resistance changes how the part looks after a year in service, not how it looks on day one. His concession is one line, with no defence of the position he just held.
  - Note the regression asymmetry: a known past field complaint (bare stainless handlebar in cold climates on the original R3 scrub) makes the baseline feature a hard requirement, while the incremental improvement on top of it has to be argued for. "Don't wanna regress" is doing the same work here as "Don't wanna get rekt from lack of mechanical safety factor" did on the hinge — a prior failure converts a nice-to-have into a floor.

- **[2026-09-02] Accepts 2–4 mm handlebar-curve deformation at DV1 and controls it with a QC jig rather than tightening the process.** Posted a "Control for handlebar curves" item to Wei Hua and Hisham; Hisham read it back as "so basically they will put it through this jig to check as QC?" and "the 2-4mm deformation we will expect for DV1?", and Benjamin confirmed both with "yea probably". Characteristic phase-scoped tolerance handling — a go/no-go gauge that catches the out-of-family parts is the DV-appropriate answer, not a bend process capable of holding the nominal. Note the hedge is left in ("probably"); he confirms the expectation without converting it into a spec. — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1788335177093539)
- **[2026-09-02] Named the one degree of freedom a screw location has, rather than answering yes or no.** Hisham asked whether a handlebar screw could be shifted inboard for SOP so it wouldn't be visible with the handlebar closed: "Screwing points cant change face, only can translate up or down the handlebar." One line that refuses the request and simultaneously hands over the full remaining design space, so Hisham can re-solve the cosmetic problem himself without another round trip. — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1788355576401489)
- **[2026-09-04] Refused extra fasteners on over-constraint grounds, and fixed a loosening joint with a consumable plus a process step instead.** Hisham brought the side-depth-camera rigidity problem here (Ryan Arya had found the camera visibly moves with the handlebar, breaking its calibration — see `s3-sensor-config-group.md`) and proposed the obvious hardware answer: two more mounting holes on each aluminium camera bracket tying it to the tank, five mounting points to the roto body in total, plus "slight mold modification to add two more brass inserts on each side." Benjamin rejected it on kinematics, not cost or schedule: "There would be issues with Double datums so it isn't possible." Then gave the fix — "Solution would be to simply use loctite here, plus torque wrench" — and, separately, the one geometry change that *would* be legitimate: "If we want to add an additional boss constraining geometry at the front instead, is possible."
  - Three distinct moves in four minutes. (1) Bolting one bracket to two independently-located bodies over-constrains it; more fasteners would fight each other rather than add stiffness, so the proposal fails on principle regardless of tooling cost. (2) With nothing already in the design able to carry the function, his next resort is **not** a part but a consumable and a process — threadlocker plus a torque spec. (3) The legitimate alternative is a *single* added constraining feature, not redundant ones. Read against the standing "no new part, no new code" pattern, this fills in what he does when neither applies: add process, not hardware.
  - Note where this sits relative to the team's own standard (`reference/torque-and-loctite.md`): the default for screws into aluminium/stainless is Loctite **and no torque wrench**, and "no torque value is given for a screw that has Loctite, unless it is really needed." He is calling for both here, i.e. exercising the standard's own exception. What triggers it is worth recording — a joint whose slow loosening degrades sensor calibration silently, without ever presenting as a loose screw. — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1788512802443759)

## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed
- **Safety factor and structural load-bearing take priority over cosmetic/packaging convenience** — repeatedly cited that the whole robot's weight rests on the handlebar and designed accordingly (dual hinge, safety factor margin), even when it costs more package space or complicates the CNC/rotomold split.
- **Willing to spend on tooling for repeatable, testable prototypes** rather than one-off 3D prints/CNC, specifically to enable destructive testing and validation — an explicit trade-off in favor of process maturity even at prototype stage.
- **Sensor-detection needs shape mechanical/cosmetic decisions** (anodizing color, geometry offsets) — flags uncertainty honestly ("by how much idk") rather than asserting false confidence.
- **Pragmatic risk acceptance over gold-plating**: accepts cosmetic imperfections (wrinkles, bend radius risk) if a downstream part (the rubber grip) can absorb/hide them, rather than always redesigning to eliminate the underlying issue.
- **Documents constraints in Slack proactively** as a memory aid for collaborators ("putting here in case you forget"), suggesting a habit of externalizing tribal knowledge rather than relying on verbal-only agreements.
