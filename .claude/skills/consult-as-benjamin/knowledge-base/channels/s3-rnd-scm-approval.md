SCM ruled that engineers supply drawings and technical communication while SCM owns price; his template carries the drawing change and the supplier contact, and never a number. The day after the last of these he wrote the rule down for everyone else (`s3-mech-architecture.md`, 2026-09-10) — note the order: he ran the practice for a day first, then codified it, the same sequence as the NPPA folder standard (2026-08-13). — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0BSFELMSLS/p1788937240048139)

Pre-empting the exact wrong conclusion the reader is about to draw, in the position where they will draw it — the same reflex as "Not a regression from this update" on the Pack-and-Go failure note (`s3-mech-cad-changelog.md`, 2026-08-29).
  - Also worth recording as engineering content rather than procurement: he accepted a **supplier-initiated process change** (powder coating → anodizing across six handlebar parts) and absorbed it as a drawing-REV bump only, and `MNT-0932`/`MNT-0933` moved X0→X1→X2 inside 30 hours — two physical revisions of the same pair of parts in a day and a half, each announced separately rather than batched.

- **[2026-09-15 → 09-18] The change-notice template continues, and two new variants appear — the drawing-REV-only bump and the correction of someone else's rollback.** The same three recipients, the same price-free shape:
  - physical change, Drive link inline, change stated in four words: "`MNT-0959-X0` ->X1 FRONT PROFILE SEAL MOUNT ... changes: Adjusted height of part" (09-15); also `MNT-0924-X0`->X1 Brush Door Mount (09-15) and `MNT-0945-X0`->X1 Side Brush Adapter (09-15, with "updated in GOOGLE DRIVE" in caps).
  - **paperwork-only, stated as such up front**: "Drawing REV number increased 01->02, NO CHANGE in CAD. New drawings sent to supplier. G Drive updated." for `MNT-0930`/`MNT-0931` Handlebar Mount Left/Right (09-18). This is exactly the distinction his own 2026-09-10 rule prescribes, executed in his own notices — the REV moves, the X-suffix does not, and the reader is told in the first clause which kind of change this is so they know whether to re-check geometry.
  - **a correction of a downstream system, not of himself**: "`MNT-0947-X0` -> `MNT-0947-X1` Quick Release Latch. This one has been reverted in the BOM to X0. We have corrected it to X1." (09-17) — the revision had regressed in the BOM, he states the fact and the fix in two sentences without asking who did it.
- **[2026-09-15] Scoped tooling spend to the build phase, in three fragments.** Asked by Boon Seong about tooling for a DV part: "No need tooling for this small DV batch. Is only CNC. Tooling is only for PV or SOP." The rule and its exception in eleven words — a DV batch is small enough that CNC amortises better than a tool, and tooling investment is gated to the phases where volume justifies it.
- **[2026-09-15] Would not approve a fixture spend he could not trace to a physical cause — and reconstructed what the supplier probably meant from his own design change.** A supplier request came through SCM about reworking a fixture. He first refused to answer the question as asked: "Wait, he is talking about gasket parts, not side brushes?" → "What sku?" → "I really do not know what they are talking about, unless the translation has issues, he may be talking about the jig to tuft the bristles in, because the bristle angle did change, so that will need a new fixture" → "Nothing to do with any gaskets." Once the subject was pinned down he ruled immediately: "Okay then yes, new fixture needed for new angle."
  - Two things are characteristic. He treats a confusing supplier message as **probably a translation problem plus a wrong part reference**, and resolves it by asking what design change of his own could have caused a fixture request at all — the bristle angle changed, therefore the tufting jig is the thing that needs remaking. He reasons from his own recent change to the supplier's likely meaning rather than asking them to re-explain.
  - And on the rework-vs-new-fixture question he declined to guess and converted it into a test for the supplier: "I am not sure what the fixture is for. Best is to make a new fixture. Do you know what is the downside for reworking the fixture?" → "I'm not sure the risk of reworking actually, ask them if they can maintain the new angle as per the drawing if it is reworked?" His default is the clean-sheet fixture, but he will accept rework if the supplier will commit to the drawing spec — the acceptance criterion is the drawing, not their opinion. Plain admission of his own uncertainty twice ("I am not sure", "I'm not sure the risk") without it weakening the ruling.
