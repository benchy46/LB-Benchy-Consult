# #s3-handlebar
**Slack channel ID:** C0B2SFZAQKA
**Purpose:** Working channel dedicated to designing the S3 robot's handlebar assembly (hinge mechanism, extrusion profile, grip material, manufacturing) — created by Jack Dyer on 2026-05-07 from a conversation with Benjamin Chay, Benjamin Ang, and Hisham Baharin. Explicitly contrasted with MT1's competitor design (8 screws, one side only, secured to the rotomold) and said "idk how to make it strong enough" that way, and separately "you can't even lift the robot with that handlebar design" referring to a push-only mechanism — 2026-05-14 & 2026-05-25 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1779704954292029) / [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1779705106310779)

- **Rear clearance requirement** — specified needing "30mm or more towards the rear of the robot, from the rotation axis of the handlebar" for his mechanism — 2026-05-12 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1778553723040259)
- **Rubber molded grip, snap-fit and tool-free replaceable, over a fixed/screwed part** — "My intent is to have a rubber molded part instead. And not even screwed on, but held by only a specific geometry that constrains all DOF. It is cheaper and easy to replace if damaged." Traded this off candidly against a LiDAR-detection risk he flagged rather than hid: "Offset will affect lidar scan reliability of handlebar detection, by how much idk" — 2026-06-04 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0B2SFZAQKA/p1780540961459159)

I would order more for validation and even destructive testing." When Pradeep asked about the risk of profile changes forcing a new mold, he accepted "we pay for bending mold change. Extrusion mold can keep. Can't think of a better option here." — full thread with Daniel Tan and Pradeep Kumar — 2026-05-21 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C0AAGTENGJK/p1779336920491659) (posted in #s3-mech-architecture, directly about the handlebar)

Yes sandblast confirm") specifically so the 3D LiDAR could detect the handlebar in its raised position, over a plain black anodize; left open whether a reflective/silver-line coating was needed, and was willing to send the supplier a RAL value or physical color samples to match — 2026-05-21 — same thread as above

## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed
- **Safety factor and structural load-bearing take priority over cosmetic/packaging convenience** — repeatedly cited that the whole robot's weight rests on the handlebar and designed accordingly (dual hinge, safety factor margin), even when it costs more package space or complicates the CNC/rotomold split.
- **Willing to spend on tooling for repeatable, testable prototypes** rather than one-off 3D prints/CNC, specifically to enable destructive testing and validation — an explicit trade-off in favor of process maturity even at prototype stage.
- **Sensor-detection needs shape mechanical/cosmetic decisions** (anodizing color, geometry offsets) — flags uncertainty honestly ("by how much idk") rather than asserting false confidence.
- **Pragmatic risk acceptance over gold-plating**: accepts cosmetic imperfections (wrinkles, bend radius risk) if a downstream part (the rubber grip) can absorb/hide them, rather than always redesigning to eliminate the underlying issue.
- **Documents constraints in Slack proactively** as a memory aid for collaborators ("putting here in case you forget"), suggesting a habit of externalizing tribal knowledge rather than relying on verbal-only agreements.
