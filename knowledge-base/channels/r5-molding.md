## Decisions & Reasoning (Benjamin)
- **Pushed back on a supplier's conservative draft-angle spec using industry-standard knowledge**: "Kaier has requested draft angle of 2 deg for VDI 18, when industrial standards say 1 deg is enough (maybe skill issue, last time they not like that), so I've been dropping my parts down to VDI 15 instead, for parts that are complicated to change the draft angle." He explicitly floated that the supplier's ask might reflect a capability gap rather than a real requirement, and adjusted his own parts' texture spec (VDI level) downward rather than accept the extra draft angle by default — 2026-01-02 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C09UUMRQQAK/p1767362906768039)
- **Established a documentation discipline for insert specs to prevent the supplier defaulting to the cheapest option**: "if your part has inserts, usually the specific dimensions of the hole doesn't matter, you can take the gauge from the notion page of the OD of inserts that Kaier uses, and offset your wall thickness... Also do indicate in your drawings the thread length, AND if your inserts should be Cold pressed or Molded in. Or else kaier will just choose the cheaper option." Reflects an explicit assumption that an under-specified drawing will be resolved by the supplier in the supplier's favor (cost) rather than the design's favor — 2025-12-04 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C09UUMRQQAK/p1764837810769519)
- **Set a standard pre-upload checklist and default material palette** for all part owners (material, colour, texture, draft angle, wall thickness, fillets, date stamp, drawing notes, correct SKU), with default materials specified by use case: PA-765A for normal plastic, NH-1013U for UL-rated/high-strength electronics housings, PVC for chemical resistance, PC-940A for translucent parts — a reusable decision framework rather than case-by-case material picks — 2025-12-02 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C09UUMRQQAK/p1764649191649539)
- **Created a two-tier "Molding Needed" vs "Molding Needed Priority" triage classification** given supplier capacity constraints: "Kaier may not be able to make every part in time, therefore Lucas and I will classify certain plastic parts as Molding Needed Priority in the BOM... it may or may not affect family molding and the quotation." Tied the classification to a concrete deadline (mid-Feb FOT for first-week-March production) and left room for the supplier to expand the priority list if they had confidence to hit the deadline — 2025-12-05 — [source](https://lionsbot.slack.com/archives/C09UUMRQQAK/p1764898621935289)

## Recurring Themes / Principles Observed
- Treats supplier defaults (draft angle, insert type, cost-optimized choices) with suspicion when unspecified — assumes the supplier will pick the cheaper or easier option unless the drawing/spec explicitly constrains it, and documents accordingly.
- Willing to second-guess a supplier's technical requirement against known industry standards rather than accept it at face value, framing it explicitly as a possible "skill issue" on the supplier's side.
- Uses simple two-tier triage/classification schemes (priority vs non-priority parts) to manage schedule risk under supplier capacity constraints, rather than trying to expedite everything uniformly.
- When a design requirement collides with a manufacturing constraint (sink marks), prefers finding an alternate way to preserve the original requirement over dropping it.
